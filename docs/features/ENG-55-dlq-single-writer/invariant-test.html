Scope & Baseline

  • Invariants in scope: ADR-0003 (R/W single sync point) restated load-bearing at internal/logical.go:100-108, plus the four Correctness Constraints in the ENG-55 blueprint (single-writer, accounting-matches-storage, delete-after-confirm, prompt-wake) and the deadlock-freedom precondition (validateDeadQueue).
  • Governed resources: (a) the dead-letter storage partition; (b) the dead-letter Logical's in-memory QueueState / Partition.State.UnLeased / Lifecycle.NextLifecycleRun; (c) source-partition items pending a dead-letter move.
  • Defensive mechanisms found: per-backend partition mutex (e.g. MemoryPartition lock) and individually-atomic backend Produce — these guarantee integrity of a single partition write regardless of which goroutine calls it. This is precisely why an integrity-only suite stayed green on the buggy main.
  • Surface used: QueueProduce, QueueLease, QueueStats (incl. the new unLeased / nextLifecycleRun partition fields), StorageItemsList, PauseQueue — all driven through the client. No internal calls.
  • Baseline: go test ./service/ -race on DeadLetterQueue/SingleWriter — green before mutation, green after revert.

The masking lesson (why -race + integrity tests missed ENG-55)

The single-writer bypass and a data race look identical at the diff, but the partition mutex makes the bypass memory-safe and integrity-preserving: under the reintroduced bypass the dead-lettered item was still durably written to storage. So every assertion of the form "the item is in the dead-letter queue / not lost / not duplicated" passes on broken code. The only assertions that fail are the ones the mutex cannot satisfy — the consequences of the single writer also being the thing that wakes waiters and advances the in-memory timer in the same pass: liveness and timeliness/accounting. Those are the kept assertions below.
